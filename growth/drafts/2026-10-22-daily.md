> An options interview question that takes 30 seconds if you know one equation, and forever if you don't.
>
> A 1-year European call on a non-dividend stock costs $10.45. Stock at $100, strike $100, rate 5% a year.
> What must the matching put cost?
>
> You don't need volatility. You don't need Black-Scholes. You need put-call parity:
>
> C + PV(K) = P + S
>
> Both sides pay exactly the same at expiry, so they must cost the same today. If they didn't, you'd buy the cheap side, sell the dear side and lock in a risk-free profit.
>
> PV(K) = 100 / 1.05 = 95.24
> P = 10.45 + 95.24 − 100 = $5.69
>
> The point interviewers are checking: parity is a no-arbitrage result, not a pricing model. It holds whatever volatility you assume.
>
> On Basis every learner gets their own numbers for this problem, so you can't copy an answer. You have to work it out. It's free:
