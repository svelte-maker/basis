> "Why do you subtract cash to get enterprise value?"
>
> It's one of the most common valuation interview questions, and "because the formula says so" is the wrong answer.
>
> Enterprise value is the price of the whole business: what you'd pay to own the operations, whoever funded them.
>
> Buy every share and you also take on the debt, so add it. You also get the cash in the bank, which effectively refunds part of the price, so subtract it.
>
> A worked example:
> Share price $50 × 100m diluted shares = $5,000m equity value
> + Debt $1,500m
> − Cash $500m
> = EV of $6,000m
>
> The follow-up that separates candidates: the company owns 80% of a subsidiary and consolidates 100% of its EBITDA. What changes?
> You add the $200m minority interest, so EV = $6,200m. EBITDA includes 100% of the subsidiary, so EV has to cover 100% of it too, or your EV/EBITDA is understated.
>
