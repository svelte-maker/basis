## 1. LinkedIn

- **Goal:** a useful Saturday learning post that sends people to the time value of money lesson.
- **Suggested time:** Sat 24 Oct, 11:00 IST
- **Link:** https://basis-psi-roan.vercel.app/learn/time-value-of-money

> "12% a year" is not one number.
>
> Put ₹1,00,000 in at 12% for a year:
> Compounded annually, you end with ₹1,12,000.
> Compounded monthly (1% a month), you end with ₹1,12,683.
>
> Same "12%", different money. The rate you're quoted is nominal. The rate you actually earn (or pay) is the effective annual rate:
>
> EAR = (1 + r/m)^m − 1
>
> 12% nominal works out to:
> • 12.000% compounded annually
> • 12.551% quarterly
> • 12.683% monthly
> • 12.747% daily
>
> Where it really bites: a card charging 3.5% a month is a 42% nominal rate, but the effective rate is 51.11%.
>
> Comparing two loans, two FDs or two bonds? Convert both to EAR first.
>
> The full lesson, with an editable spreadsheet (free):
> https://basis-psi-roan.vercel.app/learn/time-value-of-money
