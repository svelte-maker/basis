- **Goal:** launch the new lesson with a number that surprises people.
- **Suggested time:** Mon 26 Oct, 18:30 IST (or Tue 27 Oct, 09:00 IST if the PR merges late)
- **Link:** https://basis-psi-roan.vercel.app/learn/private-equity-fund-waterfall

> A private equity fund turns $100m into $200m in five years. The investors get back $180m.
>
> Where did the other $20m go? To the manager, as carried interest, paid out through the "distribution waterfall".
>
> Here's the standard whole-fund version, tier by tier:
>
> 1️⃣ Return of capital: investors get their $100m back.
> 2️⃣ Preferred return: investors get 8% a year, compounded. Over 5 years that's $46.93m.
> 3️⃣ Catch-up: the manager takes 100% of the next $11.73m, until it holds 20% of the profit paid so far.
> 4️⃣ Split: the last $41.33m goes 80/20.
>
> Manager's total: $20.00m, exactly 20% of the $100m profit.
>
> The part most people get wrong: the 8% hurdle didn't reduce the manager's pay at all. With a full catch-up, the hurdle decides *whether* the manager gets carry, not *how much* once the fund does well.
>
> Below the hurdle it's a different story. If the fund returns $130m, the manager gets nothing.
>
> We wrote a free lesson that walks through every tier, with a live spreadsheet you can change:
> https://basis-psi-roan.vercel.app/learn/private-equity-fund-waterfall

**Title:** The PE distribution waterfall, with numbers (the catch-up is the part interviewers test)

**Body:**
> The waterfall comes up a lot in PE and fund interviews, and the catch-up is where most explanations get vague. Here's one worked through with real numbers in case it helps someone.
>
> **Set-up:** LPs put in $100m. After 5 years the fund distributes $200m. Whole-fund (European) waterfall: 8% preferred return compounded annually, 100% GP catch-up, 20% carry. Fees ignored.
>
> **Tier 1, return of capital:** LPs get $100m back. $100m left.
>
> **Tier 2, preferred return:** $100m × (1.08⁵ − 1) = $46.93m to LPs. $53.07m left.
>
> **Tier 3, catch-up:** GP takes 100% until it has 20% of all profit paid so far. LPs have $46.93m of profit, so the GP needs 20/80 of that: $11.73m. $41.33m left.
>
> **Tier 4, split:** $41.33m goes 80/20. GP gets $8.27m more.
>
> **GP total: $20.00m**, which is exactly 20% of the $100m profit. LPs end with $180m: 1.8x net against 2.0x gross.
>
> **Things that tripped me up:**
> 1. With a full catch-up, the hurdle doesn't reduce the GP's carry once the fund does well. It only decides whether the GP gets carry at all.
> 2. In between, it isn't 20%. At $155m of proceeds the GP gets $8.07m, which is 14.7% of profit, because the whole remainder sits inside the catch-up.
> 3. Below the hurdle the GP gets nothing. At $130m, the GP gets $0.
> 4. The catch-up is 20/80 of the preferred return, not 20% of it. Using 20% is the most common slip.
> 5. Deal-by-deal (American) waterfalls run this per exit, so the GP can be paid on early winners. That's why those funds have clawback and escrow.
>
> Corrections welcome if your fund's LPA does it differently. Partial catch-ups (like 50/50) and tiered carry change the numbers.
