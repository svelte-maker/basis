## 1. LinkedIn

- **Goal:** a ratio question that analysts and students both save.
- **Suggested time:** Tue 27 Oct, 17:30 IST
- **Link:** https://basis-psi-roan.vercel.app/learn/ratio-analysis

> Two companies. Both earn an 18% return on equity. Which would you rather own?
>
> Company A: ₹1,000 crore of revenue, ₹90 crore of profit.
> Company B: ₹3,000 crore of revenue, ₹45 crore of profit.
>
> Same ROE, so they look alike. Split ROE into its three parts (DuPont) and they're nothing alike:
>
> ROE = net margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier
>
> A: 9% × 1.0x × 2.0x = 18%
> B: 1.5% × 4.0x × 3.0x = 18%
>
> A earns its return from **pricing power**: a fat margin on a modest asset base.
> B earns it from **speed and debt**: thin margins, assets turned over four times a year, and more borrowed money.
>
> Now take away B's extra leverage. At A's 2.0x equity multiplier, B's ROE falls to 12%. A third of its return was the balance sheet, not the business.
>
> That's the question DuPont makes you ask: is a high ROE coming from the business or from the borrowing?
>
