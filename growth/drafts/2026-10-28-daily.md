> The best way to study finance with a friend: make sure you can't copy each other.
>
> Take the same NPV question in two versions.
>
> Version 1: a project costs 1,000 and pays 400, 450 and 500 over three years. Discount at 10%.
> Version 2: a project costs 1,200 and pays 500, 450 and 600. Discount at 8%.
>
> Both make 350 of profit before discounting. Their NPVs are different:
> Version 1: 363.64 + 371.90 + 375.66 − 1,000 = **111.19**
> Version 2: 462.96 + 385.80 + 476.30 − 1,200 = **125.06**
>
> If you and a friend each get one, comparing answers tells you nothing. You have to compare *how* you got there: did you discount year 3 by (1.10)³, did you subtract the cost at year 0 undiscounted?
>
> That's how practice works on Basis. Every learner gets their own numbers for the same problem, so study groups talk about method, which is what an exam or interview actually tests.
>
