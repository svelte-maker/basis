## 1. LinkedIn

- **Goal:** correct a common misconception with one clean example.
- **Suggested time:** Thu 29 Oct, 17:30 IST
- **Link:** https://basis-psi-roan.vercel.app/learn/options-and-forwards

> A forward price is not the market's forecast.
>
> A stock trades at ₹2,000. The one-year risk-free rate is 6.5%. The stock pays no dividend.
>
> The one-year forward price is ₹2,000 × 1.065 = **₹2,130**. That holds whether analysts think the stock is going to ₹1,500 or ₹3,000.
>
> Why? Because you can build the forward yourself. Borrow ₹2,000, buy the stock today and hold it. In a year you own the stock and owe ₹2,130. That's the price.
>
> If someone offers a forward at ₹2,150, you sell it to them, borrow and buy the stock, and in a year you deliver the stock, collect ₹2,150 and repay ₹2,130. That's **₹20** a share, with no view on the market at all.
>
> So a forward price tells you about interest rates and carry, not where the price is going.
>
