**Where the numbers come from:** `creditRwa` and `capitalRatio` in `src/lib/finance/banking.ts`, through the lesson's worked example:

| Exposure ($m) | Amount | Risk weight | RWA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Government bonds (AAA) | 300 | 0% | 0 |
| Residential mortgages | 400 | 35% | 140 |
| Retail loans | 200 | 75% | 150 |
| Corporate loans (unrated) | 500 | 100% | 500 |
| **Credit RWA** | 1,400 | | **790** |

Add market and operational RWA of 110 to get a total RWA of 900. CET1 is 81, so the **CET1 ratio is 9.00%**.
- **Move 200 from corporate loans to mortgages:** RWA falls to 770 and the ratio rises to **10.52%**, with the same capital.
- **Write off 18 of corporate loans:** capital falls to 63 and RWA to 882, so the ratio drops to **7.14%**.
- **Divide by exposures instead of RWA:** 81 ÷ 1,400 = 5.79%, the most common mistake.

**Title:** CET1 ratio explained with one small bank (for anyone with a bank or risk interview coming up)

**Body:**
> This comes up in a lot of commercial banking, credit risk and FIG interviews, and most explanations stop at "capital ÷ risk-weighted assets". Here it is worked through end to end.
>
> **The bank ($m)**
>
> | Exposure | Amount | Risk weight | RWA |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Government bonds (AAA) | 300 | 0% | 0 |
> | Residential mortgages | 400 | 35% | 140 |
> | Retail loans | 200 | 75% | 150 |
> | Corporate loans (unrated) | 500 | 100% | 500 |
>
> Credit RWA = 790. Add 110 for market and operational risk, so total RWA = 900. CET1 capital = 81.
>
> **CET1 ratio = 81 / 900 = 9.00%.** Basel III requires 4.5%, plus a 2.5% conservation buffer. Inside the buffer, payouts get restricted. Below 4.5% is a breach.
>
> **Three things interviewers like to ask:**
>
> 1. *How do you raise the ratio without raising capital?* Shift the mix. Move 200 from corporate loans to mortgages, and RWA falls to 770 while the ratio rises to 10.52%. Same assets, same capital.
> 2. *What does a loss do?* Write off 18 of corporate loans. Capital falls to 63 but RWA only falls to 882, so the ratio drops to 7.14%. Losses hit the numerator far harder than the denominator.
> 3. *Why is there also a leverage ratio?* Because risk weights can be wrong. Tier 1 ÷ total exposure, with no weights and a 3% minimum, is the backstop. The classic mistake is mixing the two up: 81 / 1,400 = 5.79% is not the CET1 ratio.
>
> Caveat: the 35% mortgage weight is the old standardised one. Under the 2017 Basel III reforms it depends on LTV (20% to 70%), and big banks use internal models with a 72.5% output floor.
>
> Happy to answer follow-ups.
