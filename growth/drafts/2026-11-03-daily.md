**Where the numbers come from:** the "Cash conversion cycle" ratio in `computeRatios` (`src/lib/finance/ratios.ts`, DSO + DIO − DPO), for one example company with figures in ₹ crore:

| Item | Value | Days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | 1,200 | |
| Cost of goods sold | 800 | |
| Receivables | 200 | DSO = 200 ÷ 1,200 × 365 = 60.83 |
| Inventory | 160 | DIO = 160 ÷ 800 × 365 = 73.00 |
| Payables | 120 | DPO = 120 ÷ 800 × 365 = 54.75 |
| **Cash conversion cycle** | | **79.08 days** |

Working capital is 200 + 160 − 120 = ₹240 crore.
- **Grow 25% with the same days** (revenue 1,500, COGS 1,000): the CCC stays at 79.08 days, but working capital rises to ₹300 crore, so growth eats ₹60 crore of cash.
- **Collect 15 days faster:** receivables fall by 1,200 × 15 ÷ 365 = ₹49.32 crore, and the CCC drops to 64.08 days.

## 1. LinkedIn

- **Goal:** a saveable explainer for students and early-career analysts on why profit isn't cash.
- **Suggested time:** Tue 3 Nov, 17:30 IST
- **Link:** https://basis-psi-roan.vercel.app/learn/working-capital

> Growth can drain a company's cash.
>
> Take a company with ₹1,200 crore of revenue:
>
> • Customers pay in about 61 days (receivables of ₹200 crore)
> • Stock sits for 73 days (inventory of ₹160 crore)
> • It pays suppliers in about 55 days (payables of ₹120 crore)
>
> Cash conversion cycle = 61 + 73 − 55 = **79 days**. That's how long each rupee is stuck between paying for stock and collecting from the customer.
>
> Now it grows 25%, with the same terms. The cycle is still 79 days, but working capital rises from ₹240 crore to ₹300 crore. **₹60 crore of cash disappears into the business**, even if every sale is profitable.
>
> The fix usually isn't more sales. It's fewer days. Collecting 15 days faster frees about ₹49 crore.
>
> That's why lenders and analysts read the days, not just the margin.
>
