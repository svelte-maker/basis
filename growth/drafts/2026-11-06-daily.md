**Where the numbers come from:** `accretionDilution` in `src/lib/finance/corporate.ts`, with the same deal the `/learn/mergers-accretion-dilution` lesson uses. The acquirer earns $500m on 100m shares (EPS $5.00) and trades at $60 (P/E 12). It buys a target earning $80m for $1,200m (purchase P/E 15). Debt costs 8% pre-tax, 25% tax rate, no synergies:

| How it pays | New shares | Pro forma EPS | Accretion / dilution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100% stock | 20m | $4.83 | −3.33% |
| 50% stock, 50% cash | 10m | $4.95 | −1.09% |
| 100% cash (debt) | 0 | $5.08 | +1.60% |

The shortcuts: in an all-stock deal, buying a company on a higher P/E (15) than your own (12) dilutes EPS. In an all-cash deal, it's accretive if the target's earnings yield (80 / 1,200 = 6.67%) beats the after-tax cost of debt (8% × 0.75 = 6.00%). If the price rises to $1,500m, the earnings yield drops to 5.33% and even the all-cash deal dilutes (−2.00%). The `eps-accretion-dilution` problem isn't on any contest list, and no contest problem is used.

> Same deal. Same price. Pay with stock and EPS falls. Pay with cash and EPS rises.
>
> A company earns $500m on 100m shares (EPS $5.00) and trades at a P/E of 12. It buys a target earning $80m for $1,200m, a P/E of 15. Debt costs 8% before tax, and the tax rate is 25%.
>
> • All stock: 20m new shares, EPS **$4.83 (−3.33%)**
> • Half and half: EPS **$4.95 (−1.09%)**
> • All cash: EPS **$5.08 (+1.60%)**
>
> Two shortcuts give you the answer before any maths:
>
> **Stock:** buying earnings at a higher P/E (15) than your own (12) dilutes.
> **Cash:** it's accretive if the target's earnings yield (80 / 1,200 = 6.67%) beats the after-tax cost of debt (6.00%).
>
> And the follow-up interviewers love: accretive doesn't mean the deal creates value. Cash deals add leverage and risk that EPS doesn't show.
