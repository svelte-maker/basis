## 1. LinkedIn

- **Goal:** a clear markets explainer for students and early-career readers.
- **Suggested time:** Thu 12 Nov, 17:30 IST
- **Link:** https://basis-psi-roan.vercel.app/learn/bonds-and-yields

> The yield curve contains a forecast most people never read.
>
> Say a 1-year deposit pays 6% and a 2-year deposit pays 7% a year.
>
> You can lock in 2 years at 7%, or do 1 year at 6% and roll over. For rolling to match locking in, next year's 1-year rate has to be:
>
> 1.07² ÷ 1.06 − 1 = **8.01%**
>
> That's the **forward rate**: the rate the curve implies for a future period. ₹1 lakh at 7% for two years becomes ₹1,14,490. Rolling at 6% and then 8.01% gets you to the same place.
>
> How to use it:
> • If you think rates next year will be **above** 8.01%, rolling over wins.
> • If you think they'll be **below**, locking in wins.
>
> Flip the curve (1-year 7%, 2-year 6.5%) and the implied forward drops to **6.00%**. An inverted curve is the market pricing in lower rates ahead.
>
> (Rates are illustrative, and forwards are a breakeven, not a guaranteed forecast.)

**Reply to Post A (post about 2 hours later):**
> 8.01%. 1.07² = 1.1449, and 1.1449 ÷ 1.06 = 1.0801. That's the 1-year forward rate. It's often quoted as "about 8%", because 2 × 7% − 6% = 8% is the quick approximation; compounding adds the extra 0.01%.
