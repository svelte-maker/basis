> A 7% fixed deposit can make you poorer.

> Here's the maths, with illustrative numbers:
>
> FD rate: 7%
> Tax at the 30% slab: 7% × 0.7 = 4.90% left
> Inflation: 6%
>
> Real return = 1.049 / 1.06 − 1 = **−1.04% a year.**
>
> Over 10 years:
> → ₹1,00,000 grows to ₹1,61,345.
> → Whatever costs ₹1,00,000 today costs ₹1,79,085.
> → In today's rupees, your deposit is worth about ₹90,094.
>
> The balance went up 61%. What it can buy went down about 10%.
>
> Three takeaways that show up everywhere in finance:
> 1. Always ask "real or nominal?" A return means little until you subtract inflation.
> 2. Ask "before or after tax?" Here tax turned a small real gain (+0.94%) into a loss.
> 3. The quick shortcut (4.90% − 6% = −1.10%) is close, but the exact formula divides, it doesn't subtract.
>
> At these assumptions, an FD would need to pay 8.57% just to keep up.
