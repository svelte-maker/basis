**Link:** https://basis-psi-roan.vercel.app/learn/real-vs-nominal-returns-inflation

> ₹1 lakh, 20 years from now, will buy what ₹31,180 buys today.
>
> That's 6% inflation a year (an illustrative rate), compounded. At that pace, money loses half its purchasing power roughly every 12 years.
>
> Three places this quietly changes the answer:
>
> 1. **Goals.** A ₹50 lakh target in today's money isn't ₹50 lakh in 20 years. Plan in today's rupees, then grow the target by inflation.
>
> 2. **Returns.** Real return = (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. At 10% and 4%, that's 5.77%, not 6%. At 100% and 80%, it's 11.11%, not 20%. Divide, don't subtract.
>
> 3. **Valuation.** Discount real cash flows at a real rate, or nominal at nominal. Both give 277.51 in our example. Mix them and you get 311.69 or 247.64.
>

## 5. Reddit (the week's post)

**Subreddit:** r/personalfinanceindia (last week's draft was for r/IndiaInvestments; rotate).
**Goal:** a useful explainer that stands on its own; profile visits, not clicks.
**Suggested time:** Tue 17 Nov, 19:30 IST.
**Rules:** no link in the post body. If someone asks for the calculator, reply with the link and disclose: "I help build this site."

**Title:** How to work out the real return on an FD after tax and inflation (with the exact formula, not the shortcut)

> Most FD comparisons stop at the interest rate. Here's how to get to the number that matters, with illustrative rates. Plug in your own.
>
> **1. Take off tax.** Interest is taxed at your slab rate. At 7% and the 30% slab: 7% × 0.7 = 4.90%.
>
> **2. Take off inflation, the exact way.** Real return = (1 + after-tax return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. At 6% inflation: 1.049 ÷ 1.06 − 1 = −1.04% a year. (The shortcut 4.90% − 6% = −1.10% is close at these rates but drifts at higher ones.)
>
> **3. See it in rupees.** ₹1,00,000 for 10 years at 4.90% becomes ₹1,61,345. A basket that costs ₹1,00,000 today costs ₹1,79,085 then. In today's money, the deposit is worth ₹90,094.
>
> **4. Find your break-even.** The FD rate that just keeps up after tax is inflation ÷ (1 − slab). At 6% and 30%: 8.57%. At the 20% slab: 7.50%.
>
> Notes: these are assumptions, not a forecast, and cess/surcharge are ignored. Tax treatment differs by product, so this doesn't mean FDs are bad; it means compare products on after-tax real returns. Happy to be corrected on anything.

(20% slab break-even: 6% ÷ 0.8 = 7.50%. All other figures are in the lesson.)
