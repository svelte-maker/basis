> Two companies. Both have a current ratio of 2.0x. Textbook answer: both are comfortable.
>
> Look inside current assets (₹ crore, illustrative):
>
> Company A: cash 120, receivables 150, inventory 30
> Company B: cash 20, receivables 40, inventory 240
>
> Both owe ₹150 crore within a year.
>
> Now take out inventory, which can't pay a supplier until someone buys it:
>
> Quick ratio A: (300 − 30) ÷ 150 = 1.8x
> Quick ratio B: (300 − 240) ÷ 150 = 0.4x
>
> B has ₹60 crore of fairly liquid assets against ₹150 crore of bills, and its inventory sits for about 97 days before it sells. A turns its stock in about 12.
>
> Same current ratio. Very different month-end.
>
> What credit analysts actually check:
> → Quick ratio and cash ratio, not just the current ratio
> → Days of inventory: is the stock moving or piling up?
> → Whether receivables are collectible
