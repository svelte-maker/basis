> Interview question: "You've built a DCF. Do you discount free cash flow at WACC or at the cost of equity?"
>
> The answer is "it depends which free cash flow."
>
> One company, one year (illustrative, ₹ crore):
> EBIT 200 · tax 25% · D&A 40 · capex 60 · working capital up 20
>
> **FCFF** (to all investors) = 200 × 0.75 + 40 − 60 − 20 = **110**
>
> Now take out what belongs to lenders: interest of 30 (22.5 after tax), and add any new borrowing.
>
> **FCFE** (to shareholders only):
> → Borrows 10: 110 − 22.5 + 10 = **97.5**
> → Repays 25: 110 − 22.5 − 25 = **62.5**
>
> The rule:
> FCFF → discount at WACC → enterprise value
> FCFE → discount at cost of equity → equity value
>
> Mix them and you either count the lenders' cash as the shareholders' or discount the shareholders' cash at a rate that includes cheap debt. Both overvalue the shares.
>
