> One question to ask before you use any annuity formula: when does the first payment land?
>
> ₹1 lakh a year for 5 years, discounted at 8% (illustrative):
>
> → Paid at the end of each year: worth **₹3,99,271** today
> → Paid at the start of each year: worth **₹4,31,213** today
>
> Same five payments. ₹31,942 apart.
>
> The textbook formula, C × [1 − (1 + r)^−n] / r, assumes end-of-period payments (an ordinary annuity: loan EMIs, bond coupons). Rent, insurance premiums and school fees are usually paid in advance (an annuity due).
>
> The fix is one multiplication: annuity due = ordinary annuity × (1 + r). Every payment arrives one period earlier, so the whole stream is worth 8% more here.
>
> In Excel, it's the last argument of PV and FV: 0 for end, 1 for start. Leave it blank and Excel assumes end.
>
