> Your SIP statement says +6.67%. Your actual annual return is closer to 12.5%.
>
> Illustrative example: ₹5,000 on the 1st of every month for a year. ₹60,000 in, ₹64,000 now.
>
> → Absolute return: 4,000 ÷ 60,000 = **6.67%**
> → XIRR: **12.48%** a year
>
> Why the gap? The first instalment was invested for 12 months. The last one, for 1. On average, each rupee was in the market for about 6.5 months. Earning 6.67% in about half a year is roughly 12.5% a year.
>
> A ₹60,000 lump sum making the same ₹4,000 in a full year? That really is 6.67%.
>
> Over three years it gets worse. ₹1,80,000 in, ₹2,20,000 now. "CAGR on the total" says 6.92%. The XIRR is 13.44%.
>
> Two rules:
> 1. For a SIP, use XIRR, not CAGR. In Excel: =XIRR(cash flows, dates), instalments negative, today's value positive.
> 2. Under a year, ignore the XIRR. ₹10,000 becoming ₹10,300 in two months annualises to 28%. It means nothing.
>

## 5. Reddit (the week's post)

**Subreddit:** r/IndiaInvestments (last week's was r/personalfinanceindia; rotate).
**Goal:** a useful explainer that stands on its own; profile visits, not clicks.
**Suggested time:** Tue 24 Nov, 19:30 IST.
**Rules:** no link in the post body. If someone asks for the sheet, reply with the link and disclose: "I help build this site."

**Title:** Why your SIP's absolute return and XIRR are so different, and why "CAGR on the total" is wrong (worked example)

> I keep seeing SIP returns quoted as absolute returns or as CAGR on the total invested. Here's a worked example of why neither is the annual return. Illustrative numbers; plug in your own.
>
> **Setup:** ₹5,000 on the 1st of every month, Jan to Dec 2025. ₹60,000 in. Value on 1 Jan 2026: ₹64,000.
>
> **1. Absolute return** = 4,000 ÷ 60,000 = 6.67%. True, but it ignores time.
>
> **2. CAGR on the total** treats all ₹60,000 as invested on 1 Jan 2025, so it also says 6.67%. That's only right for a lump sum.
>
> **3. XIRR** uses each instalment's real date. In Excel or Sheets: dates in column A, −5000 for each instalment and +64000 for today's value in column B, then =XIRR(B1:B13, A1:A13). Answer: 12.48%.
>
> **Why ~double?** The first ₹5,000 was in for 12 months, the last for 1. Average ≈ 6.5 months. 6.67% in about half a year ≈ 12.5% a year.
>
> **Three years:** ₹1,80,000 → ₹2,20,000. Absolute 22.22%. CAGR on total 6.92%. Dividing by 3 gives 7.41%. XIRR 13.44%.
>
> **Caveats:**
> - Under a year, XIRR is noise. ₹10,000 → ₹10,300 in two months is an XIRR of ~28%.
> - To judge a SIP, compare its XIRR with the XIRR of the same instalments, on the same dates, in the index, not with the factsheet's point-to-point CAGR.
> - Add redemptions as positive cash flows on their dates.
>
> Happy to be corrected on anything.
