> Two companies buy the same ₹100 crore machine. One reports 21% more operating profit. Neither has more cash.
>
> Illustrative example: salvage value ₹10 crore, EBITDA ₹50 crore for both.
>
> Company A depreciates it over 5 years: (100 − 10) ÷ 5 = ₹18 crore a year → EBIT ₹32 crore
> Company B depreciates it over 8 years: (100 − 10) ÷ 8 = ₹11.25 crore a year → EBIT ₹38.75 crore
>
> Same machine. Same cash out. Same business. The only difference is a management estimate of useful life.
>
> How analysts catch it:
> → Gross PP&E ÷ annual depreciation gives a rough average life. Compare it with peers.
> → Read the accounting policy note for useful lives, and look for changes in estimates.
> → For asset-heavy businesses, compare EBITDA − capex, which no depreciation policy can move.
>
