>
> Illustrative example. You own 100 shares at ₹500. The company pays a ₹20 dividend.
>
> Before: 100 × ₹500 = ₹50,000
> After: the share goes ex-dividend at about ₹480. 100 × ₹480 + ₹2,000 cash = ₹50,000
>
> Same wealth. The ₹2,000 was already inside the share price; the company just handed it to you.
>
> If your slab is 30%, tax on the dividend is ₹600. Now you have ₹49,400.
>
> The case for dividends is real, though:
> → They force management to return cash instead of wasting it.
> → A steady dividend can signal confidence in future cash flows.
> → You get cash without having to sell shares.
>
> So which side are you on: dividends add value, or dividends just move it?
