> Two investments. Both expect 10% a year. Both swing about 20% a year.
>
> Put half your money in each. What's your risk?
>
> It depends entirely on how they move together (illustrative):
>
> Correlation +1.0 → 20.00% volatility
> Correlation +0.5 → 17.32%
> Correlation 0 → 14.14%
> Correlation −0.5 → 10.00%
>
> The expected return is 10% in every row. Only the risk changes.
>
> That's why diversification is called the one free lunch in finance: combining assets that don't move in lockstep cuts risk without giving up return.
>
> The catch: correlations tend to jump towards 1 in a crash, exactly when you need them low. Diversify for normal times, and stress-test for the bad ones.
>
