> A fund returns +40%, −30%, +25%, +5% over four years.
>
> Average return: 10% a year.
>
> ₹1,00,000 invested becomes ₹1,28,625.
> That is 6.5% a year, not 10%.
> At a steady 10% it would have been ₹1,46,410.
>
> The average adds returns. Your money multiplies them:
> 1.40 × 0.70 × 1.25 × 1.05 = 1.286
>
> A −30% year needs +43% just to get back to where you were. That's why swings cost you even when the average looks fine.
>
> The tip: when you compare funds, ask for the compound annual return (CAGR, or IRR/XIRR when money goes in at different times), not the average of the yearly numbers.
>
